PBM evidence map
================

Status: August 2026

This page records the evidence boundaries that Project Lumina should use when
describing photobiomodulation (PBM) research. It is intentionally conservative:
promising biology is not treated as established clinical efficacy, and evidence
from one neurological condition is not silently transferred to another.

Evidence tiers
--------------

+--------------------------------------+-----------------------------+-------------------------------------------+
| Context                              | Evidence tier               | Project Lumina interpretation             |
+======================================+=============================+===========================================+
| Peripheral PBM in pediatric CP       | Early clinical / preliminary| Feasibility and research signal; not      |
|                                      |                             | established efficacy                      |
+--------------------------------------+-----------------------------+-------------------------------------------+
| Transcranial PBM in pediatric CP     | Pre-preliminary             | Experimental only                         |
+--------------------------------------+-----------------------------+-------------------------------------------+
| tPBM for epilepsy                    | Preclinical + first human   | Safety-sensitive translational research;  |
|                                      | pilot recruiting            | no established human efficacy             |
+--------------------------------------+-----------------------------+-------------------------------------------+
| tPBM for traumatic brain injury      | Early human evidence        | Promising translational bridge; small and |
|                                      |                             | heterogeneous evidence base               |
+--------------------------------------+-----------------------------+-------------------------------------------+
| PBM for chronic post-meningitis      | Direct efficacy evidence    | Do not infer efficacy from TBI/stroke     |
| brain injury                         | not established             | literature                                |
+--------------------------------------+-----------------------------+-------------------------------------------+

Cerebral palsy
--------------

Peripheral PBM
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

A 2025 single-blinded randomized pilot trial studied 12 children/adolescents with
spastic cerebral palsy receiving active 850 nm PBM or placebo in addition to
standard rehabilitation. Outcomes improved over time in both groups, but there
were no significant between-group differences. No short-term adverse effects
were reported.

The appropriate interpretation is **preliminary feasibility/safety with a
possible signal**, not established efficacy.

Reference:

* Zöll AC et al. *Photobiomodulation in the Treatment of Spasticity in Children
  and Adolescents with Cerebral Palsy: A Controlled, Single-Blinded, Pilot
  Randomized Trial.* Disabilities. 2025;5(4):112.
  https://doi.org/10.3390/disabilities5040112

Transcranial PBM
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

A 2026 scoping review screened 345 records and included five primary pediatric
CP studies (45 children total); only two of the five applied transcranial PBM.
The review found no sham-controlled adequately blinded trials, major protocol
heterogeneity, inconsistent dosimetry reporting, and no pediatric-specific
safety framework. It classified the evidence as pre-preliminary and recommended
that tPBM remain experimental outside controlled research.

Reference:

* Jiménez A, Carrick FR. *Transcranial Photobiomodulation for Spasticity in
  Pediatric Cerebral Palsy: A Scoping Review of Neurodevelopmental
  Considerations, Treatment Protocols, Functional Outcomes, and Methodological
  Gaps.* Brain Sci. 2026;16(3):272. PMID 41892615.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13024162/

Epilepsy
--------

Animal evidence
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

In a kainic-acid rat model, 808 nm and 940 nm tPBM were associated with lower
fast-ripple and interictal epileptiform-discharge rates, milder seizure
behaviour, less hippocampal neuronal loss, and lower caspase-3 measures than
untreated KA animals. This is preclinical evidence and does not establish human
safety or efficacy.

Reference:

* You J et al. *Preventive effects of transcranial photobiomodulation on
  epileptogenesis in a kainic acid-induced rat epilepsy model.* Exp Neurol.
  2025;383:115005. PMID 39419434.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39419434/

Human evidence
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

A first open-label pilot study in adults with drug-resistant epilepsy is now
recruiting (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07145489). The study uses daily infrared PBM
and is designed to examine seizure outcomes and tolerability. As of August 2026,
no results are posted.

Reference:

* NCT07145489, *Infrared Photobiomodulation in Humans With Epilepsy*.
  https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07145489

For Lumina, epilepsy must remain a safety-gated research domain. Any future
closed-loop work would require electrophysiological seizure-state monitoring,
explicit dose constraints, adverse-event stopping logic, and formal clinical
oversight.

Traumatic brain injury
----------------------

The TBI evidence base is further along than the conditions above but remains
small and heterogeneous.

A 2024 systematic review identified six clinical studies of tPBM and chronic
TBI cognitive outcomes. It reported encouraging cognitive findings but noted
heterogeneous application parameters and a lack of sufficient randomized
controlled evidence at the time of its search.

A 2025 randomized placebo-controlled crossover study of 17 participants with
mild TBI reported improvements after active tPBM in several cognitive and
post-concussion outcomes. Notably for Project Lumina, the study also measured
oxygenated haemoglobin with fNIRS during a visual working-memory task. The small
sample means replication is still required.

References:

* Zeng J et al. *Can transcranial photobiomodulation improve cognitive function
  in TBI patients? A systematic review.* Front Psychol. 2024;15:1378570.
  PMID 38952831. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38952831/
* Lee T-L et al. *Transcranial Photobiomodulation Improves Cognitive Function,
  Post-Concussion, and PTSD Symptoms in Mild Traumatic Brain Injury.* J
  Neurotrauma. 2025. PMID 40485299.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40485299/
* Salehpour F et al. *Photobiomodulation in Acute Traumatic Brain Injury: A
  Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.* 2022. PMID 35698294.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35698294/

Post-meningitis brain injury
----------------------------

Project Lumina should not describe TBI or stroke as a direct clinical proxy for
chronic post-meningitis injury. Different acquired brain injuries can share
processes such as inflammation, altered perfusion, mitochondrial stress, and
plasticity, but mechanistic overlap does not establish treatment transfer.

The defensible statement is:

* long-term neurological sequelae can follow meningitis;
* PBM-relevant mechanisms in other acquired brain injuries provide hypotheses;
* direct PBM efficacy for chronic post-meningitis deficits is not established;
* therefore any post-meningitis programme should begin as prospective,
  measurement-first research rather than treatment delivery.

Mechanism and dosimetry
-----------------------

PBM literature often discusses cytochrome-c-oxidase (CCO), cerebral blood flow,
oxidative stress, inflammatory signalling, and plasticity. These mechanisms
remain active research topics, and the optimal wavelength, irradiance, fluence,
pulsing, treatment duration, and anatomical targeting are not generally
standardised across neurological indications.

A 2024 systematic review of brain-PBM devices found substantial variability and
incomplete reporting of stimulation parameters, supporting a core Lumina thesis:
**measurement, dosimetry, and response standardisation are themselves important
engineering problems.**

Reference:

* Fernandes F et al. *Devices used for photobiomodulation of the brain-a
  comprehensive and systematic review.* J Neuroeng Rehabil. 2024;21:53.
  PMID 38600582. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38600582/

Implication for the Lumina roadmap
----------------------------------

The research wedge is not "PBM works, therefore automate treatment." It is:

#. measure optical and physiological response reproducibly;
#. record complete stimulation dosimetry;
#. model individual response with uncertainty;
#. test prospective predictions;
#. only then evaluate safety-gated closed-loop intervention.

This is consistent with the broader :doc:`research_scope` validation ladder.

Research-use statement
----------------------

``nlcore`` is research software. It is not a medical device, does not diagnose
disease, does not recommend treatment, and does not establish that PBM is safe
or effective for any individual patient.
